# Disproportionate Road Safety Risk for Motorcyclists & Pillion Riders (Singapore)

## Topic summary

Motorcyclists and pillion riders are vastly over-represented in traffic
accidents and fatalities relative to their share of the vehicle population.
Motorcycles make up a small fraction of Singapore's total vehicle fleet, yet
motorcyclists/pillion riders account for more than half of all traffic
incidents and a large share of fatalities.

## Why it happens

1. **Inherent exposure** — no enclosing structure, no crumple zone, no
   airbags. Energy from a collision is absorbed by the rider's body.
2. **Reduced visibility / conspicuity** — small frontal area, easily lost in
   blind spots of cars, buses, lorries; especially at junctions and during
   lane changes.
3. **Physics of crashes** — high kinetic energy relative to mass; loss of
   traction (wet roads, oil, gravel) leads to slides; single-vehicle
   run-offs; rider ejection.
4. **Driver behaviour** — failure to check blind spots, "looked but failed to
   see" (LBFS) phenomenon, turning across a motorcyclist's path, opening
   doors into traffic.
5. **Motorcyclist behaviour** — speeding, lane filtering/splitting at speed,
   tailgating, fatigue, riding under influence, poor protective gear.
6. **Road/environment** — wet weather, poor lighting, road surface defects,
   complex junctions, heavy traffic density.
7. **Pillion-specific** — inexperienced pillion affecting balance, no
   independent control, inadequate helmet fit.

## Existing interventions (insufficient alone)

- Awareness campaigns, rider training (Class 2/2A/2B), helmet laws, speed
  enforcement, black-spot treatment, road design improvements.
- Persistently high rates suggest need for targeted, innovative
  interventions.

## HMW

How might we reduce the rate of traffic incidents between motorcyclists and
drivers to effectively protect motorcyclists?

## Critique: Should the main user be "Enclosed vehicle users"?

**No — but with nuance.**

- The *primary beneficiary/victim* is the motorcyclist (and pillion), who
  bears the physical harm. Designing interventions only around enclosed
  vehicle users would centre the party with the least to lose and the most
  power to harm, risking victim-blaming and solutions that burden riders.
- However, enclosed vehicle users *are* a critical secondary user: most
  collisions are multi-vehicle and driver inattention/blind-spot failure is a
  leading cause. Interventions (ADAS blind-spot detection, mirror checks,
  turn protocols, "SMIDSY" awareness) must target drivers too.
- Recommended framing: **dual-user design** — motorcyclist as primary
  beneficiary (protection, conspicuity, skill), enclosed vehicle user as
  co-responsible actor (detection, yielding, safe manoeuvres). Neither alone
  is sufficient; treating drivers as the "main user" inverts accountability
  and may reduce rider agency/safety investment.
